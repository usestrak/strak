Getting started

Strak documentation

Strak is a terminal for every tokenized stock on Solana. Next to price and volume it shows one number no one else puts on the screen: turnover, the 24 hour volume divided by the liquidity behind it. Everyone shows the price. Strak shows whether it's lying.

Stocks on the board·
Volume, 24h·
Above 12x now·
Hottest now·

Numbers in this strip are read from /api/registry when the page loads.

Why this exists

A tokenized stock on Solana trades in an AMM pool. The price you see is simply where the last trades left that pool, and the 24h volume next to it is the sum of those trades. Both numbers are easy to inflate: a wallet that swaps back and forth with itself moves volume up and nudges the price wherever it likes, and nothing on a normal price screen tells you it happened.

There is one thing a loop can't hide: how many times it had to push the pool's entire depth through itself to produce that volume. Real demand for a stock rarely turns a pool over dozens of times a day. Strak measures exactly that, for every tokenized stock on the chain, and colours the board by it.

Who it is for

  • Traders who want to know whether a price has a market behind it before they trade against it.
  • Researchers and writers who need a single, reproducible number for "this volume looks fake".
  • Builders who want the whole tokenized equity universe on Solana as one clean JSON file.

Getting started

Quick start

  1. Open the terminal. The board lists every stock, sorted by turnover, highest first.
  2. Read the colour of the row. No tint is normal trading. A violet edge means 12x to 50x. A green glow means above 50x: the volume is painted.
  3. Click a stock. The detail panel shows the verdict bar, candles, pool depth and how often the whole pool changes hands.
  4. Check the wrappers. If the same company trades under another issuer, the prices are listed side by side. A wide gap means one of the pools is not pricing the stock.
  5. Verify on chain. Every stock links to its mint and its pool on Solscan.
No account, no wallet. Strak is read-only. It never asks to connect a wallet and never places a trade.

Getting started

Reading the terminal

The board

Each row is one tokenized stock: its symbol, name and issuer dot, price, 24h change, 24h volume and turnover. The row's tint follows the turnover band. Above the table:

  • Issuer tabs: All, Backpack, xStocks, Ondo, each with its live count, and Pre-IPO on the right, which swaps the board for a separate universe rather than filtering it.
  • Search: matches symbol (NVDAx), plain ticker (NVDA) or company name.
  • Sort: Turnover, Volume, Depth (liquidity), Change, A to Z.
  • Above 12x only: hides everything in the normal band.

The tape across the top runs the 24 highest turnovers with their 24h change. The status pill shows the US equity session in New York time (pre-market, open, after hours, closed), because the stocks these tokens track only move on news and fundamentals during that session.

Method

Turnover

Turnover is the whole product in one ratio:

turnover=volume 24h÷liquidity

Both inputs are in US dollars and cover the same token over the same 24 hours, so the ratio has no unit. It reads as "how many times did the traded volume cover the depth behind the price". A turnover of 3x means the day's trades added up to three times the pool.

Turnover as time

A ratio is easy to compute and hard to feel. Strak also shows it as time: if a day has 1440 minutes and the pool turned over t times, the whole depth changed hands once every 1440 / t minutes.

Precision

Turnover is computed once per registry build and rounded to two decimals. Volume and liquidity come from the same Jupiter record, so both sides of the ratio are always from the same moment.

Method

The three bands

Every stock falls into one of three bands. The counts are live.

up to 12xNormal trading

Volume fits what the pool can carry. The whole depth changes hands no faster than every two hours.

·
12x to 50xSuspiciously hot

Volume runs far ahead of depth. It can be news or an earnings day, it can be a loop. Open the chart before trusting the price.

·
above 50xVolume is painted

The pool would have to change hands more than twice an hour, around the clock. Treat the price as unquoted.

·

Why 12x and 50x

The thresholds are heuristics, not laws of nature, and they are stated in the open so anyone can argue with them. They come from turning the ratio into time:

  • 12x is a full turn of the pool every two hours for 24 hours straight, including the hours when the underlying US market is closed and nothing new is known about the company. A busy, healthy pool can get there on a big news day; it rarely stays there.
  • 50x is a full turn every half hour, all day and all night. At that pace the same dollars would have to cross the pool again and again. Real demand for a stock does not look like that. A loop of trades does.

Values are compared with > 12 and > 50: exactly 12.00x is still normal, exactly 50.00x is still hot.

Method

Who makes the volume

Turnover says the volume does not fit the pool. It does not say who is producing it. For that, Strak reads the pool's last 300 swaps and groups them by wallet. Four numbers come out, and together they separate a busy market from a loop.

NumberWhat it meansWhat a loop looks like
Distinct walletsHow many addresses traded in the window.Few wallets carrying a lot of volume.
Top 3 shareShare of the window's volume carried by the three biggest wallets.Above half, sustained.
Bought and soldWallets that did both inside the same window, and the share of volume they carry.A round trip is what wash trading is made of.
Trades a minute, median tradeThe pace, and the size of a typical trade.A fast, even drum of near-identical small trades.
None of these is proof on its own. Market makers quote both sides for a living, so a wallet that bought and sold is doing its job, not necessarily faking. Arbitrage between two wrappers of the same stock also shows up as round trips. Read these next to turnover, not instead of it.

The same numbers are on the landing page for the hottest pools, updating every 20 seconds, and under every chart in the terminal. Through the API they come from /api/trades.

Method

What turnover can't tell you

Turnover is a screen, not a verdict from a court. Read it with these limits in mind:

  • High is not proof. Earnings, index news or a sudden re-rating can push an honest pool into the hot band for a day. Look at the chart and at the other wrappers before concluding anything.
  • Low is not a clean bill. A pool can be manipulated with little volume, for example by one large trade in a thin pool. Low turnover only says the volume is plausible.
  • Thin pools are noisy. A stock needs at least $5K of liquidity to be on the board, but near that floor a single trade moves the ratio a lot.
  • 24 hours is a window. A burst an hour ago and a steady day can produce the same number. The candles show which it was.
  • Aggregator data. Volume and liquidity are Jupiter's figures for the token across its pools. Strak does not recount swaps itself; every figure links to the chain so it can be checked.

Method

Case files

Dated snapshots from past registry runs. They are not live data, and each one carries the date of the run it came from.

On the first registry run, IONQ from Backpack Securities reported $16.5M of volume against $107K of liquidity. To produce that honestly, the whole pool would have had to change hands roughly every nine minutes for a full day. It was the highest turnover on the board by a wide margin; the next stock was around 33x.

Two days later, on 25.09.2026, the same token read 3.7x: $413K of volume on $111K of liquidity. The depth barely moved. The volume fell by a factor of forty.

Inclusion rules

A token is on the board when all of these hold:

  1. It is in Jupiter's verified token list.
  2. It carries one of the issuer tags above.
  3. It does not carry the unknown tag. Copies that reuse a real ticker are tagged this way and are dropped.
  4. It has at least $5,000 of liquidity. Below that a pool is decoration, not a market. Issuers mint many listings nobody trades; this rule removes them.
  5. It is the deepest listing of its ticker for that issuer. If an issuer has two tokens with the same ticker, the one with more liquidity stays.

Tokens tagged as stocks but without an issuer tag are not included.

Tickers and symbols

Every record carries two names. symbol is the token's own symbol (NVDAx). ticker is the plain company ticker with the issuer suffix removed (NVDA). The terminal groups wrappers of one company by ticker.

Data

Pre-IPO tokens

A second, smaller universe trades on Solana: tokens that track companies with no listed shares at all. OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX, Neuralink, Anduril, Polymarket, Kalshi, Figure AI. Two issuers compete on the same names, PreStocks and Tessera, the second prefixing its symbols with a t.

They sit on their own tab and never mix into the board, because they break the assumption everything else rests on.

A tokenized listed stockA pre-IPO token
Behind the tokenA real share, held by a custodianA claim on a company whose shares are not traded
Reference priceThe exchange price of that shareNone. The pool price is the only price
Can the price be checked?Yes, against the marketNo, there is nothing to check it against
Do two wrappers agree?Yes, within a fraction of a percentNot comparable: each issuer sets its own share fraction
This is why turnover matters more here, not less. For a listed stock, a strange price can be caught by comparing it to the exchange. For a pre-IPO token there is no such check, so how the volume relates to the pool is close to the only thing that can be verified at all.

How they are picked

Same liquidity floor of $5,000, same duplicate collapsing. The difference is the tag: a token joins this list when Jupiter tags it prestocks or tessera and it does not carry commodities. That last rule keeps out tokenized gold and silver, which are tagged as real-world assets but are not stocks in any sense.

Tokens tagged only stocks or rwa, with no issuer behind them, are in neither list.

In the API

They arrive as a separate preipo array next to equities, in exactly the same shape. count covers listed stocks only, so nothing that reads the board picks them up by accident.

JavaScript
const { equities, preipo } = await (await fetch('https://strak-six.vercel.app/api/registry')).json();

equities.length;   // listed stocks
preipo.length;     // pre-IPO, a separate universe

Data

Sources and refresh

Every number comes from a public, free API. Nothing is typed in by hand.

SourceWhat Strak takesRefresh
Jupiter verified token listThe universe, price, 24h buy and sell volume, 24h price change, liquidity, trade count, holders, organic score, iconRegistry rebuilt on request, cached 5 min
DexScreenerThe deepest pool per token, its DEX and quote asset, the pool's 24h, 6h and 1h price changeWith the registry
GeckoTerminalOHLCV candles for the terminal chart and the landing sparklinesCandles cached 45 s, sparklines 30 min

The pipeline

  1. Fetch Jupiter's verified list (one request, about 3,600 tokens).
  2. Filter by the inclusion rules and collapse duplicates.
  3. Ask DexScreener for the pairs of the remaining mints, 30 per request, and keep the deepest pair of each.
  4. Compute turnover and sort by volume.

A full build takes a few seconds. The CDN keeps each answer for five minutes, so one build serves every visitor in that window. If the upstreams fail, the site falls back to the static snapshot in /data/equities.json, and the terminal labels the board as a snapshot instead of live.

Volume and the chart are different scopes. Turnover uses Jupiter's token-level volume and liquidity. The chart and the pool link point to the single deepest pool from DexScreener. For most stocks the deepest pool carries nearly all of the trading; where it doesn't, the numbers under the chart are still the token-level ones.

Developers

API overview

The same endpoints the site runs on are open to anyone. No key, no sign up. Responses are JSON, served from the site's own origin with CDN caching.

EndpointReturnsCached
GET /api/registryThe live board5 min
GET /api/candlesOHLCV for one pool45 s
GET /api/tradesThe last swaps in a pool and who made them20 s
GET /api/sparks24 hourly closes for the landing's pools30 min
GET /data/equities.jsonStatic snapshot of the board60 s
GET /data/cases.jsonDated case files60 s
Be gentle. The upstreams behind these routes are free and rate limited. Poll the registry no faster than every few minutes; faster polling returns the same cached answer anyway.

Developers

GET /api/registry

Builds the board from Jupiter and DexScreener and returns it in the shape described in Field reference, with "live": true.

curl
curl -s https://strak-six.vercel.app/api/registry | jq '.equities | sort_by(-.turnover) | .[0:5] | map({symbol, turnover})'
JavaScript
const res = await fetch('https://strak-six.vercel.app/api/registry');
const { equities, updatedAt } = await res.json();

const hot = equities.filter((e) => e.turnover > 12);
console.log(`${hot.length} stocks above 12x as of ${new Date(updatedAt).toISOString()}`);
StatusMeaning
200The board. Cache-Control: s-maxage=300, stale-while-revalidate=900.
502{"error": "registry unavailable"}. Upstream down; fall back to /data/equities.json.

GET /api/candles

A proxy for GeckoTerminal OHLCV on Solana. It exists because GeckoTerminal answers browsers with a 429 and no CORS headers, which a browser reports as a CORS failure.

ParamDefaultRules
poolrequiredPool address, base58, 32 to 44 characters.
tokennoneMint to price in. Pass the stock's address so candles are in the stock's price, not the quote asset's.
tfhourminute, hour or day.
agg1Candles per bar, 1 to 30. tf=minute&agg=15 is 15 minute candles.
limit200Number of candles, 10 to 1000.
curl
curl -s "https://strak-six.vercel.app/api/candles?pool=<pool>&token=<mint>&tf=hour&agg=4&limit=100"

Answer: {"ohlcv": [[time, open, high, low, close, volume], …]}, newest first, time in Unix seconds, prices and volume in USD.

StatusMeaning
400bad pool, bad token or bad timeframe.
429GeckoTerminal is rate limiting. Retry after a few seconds.
502Upstream error or unreachable.

Developers

GET /api/sparks

24 hourly closes for the pools the landing page draws sparklines for: the 8 highest turnovers and the 22 most traded stocks. It takes no parameters on purpose: one constant URL means the CDN holds one answer for half an hour, and the whole site costs GeckoTerminal about 30 calls per half hour.

{
  "sparks": { "<pool>": [44.1, 44.3, …, 44.9] },   // oldest first
  "count": 25,                                  // pools answered
  "of": 25                                      // pools asked
}

When fewer than 60% of pools answered, the response is cached for one minute instead of thirty, so a rate-limited batch is not pinned. Where a pool has no candles, the landing draws a coarse line from the pool's own pc field: four real points at 24h, 6h, 1h and now.

Developers

Static files and source

/data/equities.json is the board as of the last deploy, in the same shape as the live route without live. Use it as a fallback or when you need a fixed snapshot. /data/cases.json holds the dated case files.

The whole project is open source at github.com/usestrak/strak: the site, the API routes and the scripts. Every hour a GitHub Action saves a snapshot of the whole board to history/, one file per day, and records every stock seen above 50x in history/cases.json.

To build your own snapshot locally:

shell
node scripts/build-registry.mjs

It prints the count per issuer, the total volume and every stock above 12x, and writes public/data/equities.json.

Reference

Glossary

AMM pool
A smart contract holding two assets, for example a stock token and USDC. The price is set by the ratio of the two, and every trade moves it.
Liquidity
The USD value sitting in the pools behind a token. It is how much a trade can lean on before the price moves a lot.
Volume, 24h
The USD value of all buys and sells of the token in the last 24 hours.
Turnover
Volume divided by liquidity. How many times the day's trading covered the depth.
Wash trading
Trading with yourself, or with a friendly counterparty, to create volume and move a price without real demand.
Issuer
The company that holds the real shares and mints the token that tracks them: Backpack Securities, Backed (xStocks) or Ondo.
Wrapper
One issuer's token for a company. NVDAx is the xStocks wrapper of NVIDIA.
Price gap
The difference between two wrappers of the same company. Both track the same share, so a wide gap means one pool is not pricing it.
Pre-IPO token
A token tracking a company whose shares are not publicly traded. There is no exchange price behind it, so nothing to check the pool price against.
Mint
A token's address on Solana.
