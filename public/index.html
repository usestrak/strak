One ratio tells you if the volume is real

Turnover is 24h volume divided by the liquidity in the pool. Real traders can only push a pool around so many times a day. Past that point, the volume is the same money going in circles.

Volume a pool can't carry is volume that didn't happen

Every price on a DEX is where the last trades left the pool. When those trades turn the pool over dozens of times a day, the price is a loop, not a market. Strak puts that ratio next to every price.

Your questions, answered

The full method, data sources and API are in the documentation.

What is Strak?

A terminal for every tokenized stock on Solana: xStocks, Ondo and Backpack Securities on one screen. Next to price and volume it shows turnover, the one number that tells you whether that volume could be real.

Where do the numbers come from?

Price, 24h volume and liquidity come from Jupiter's verified token list. Pools come from DexScreener, candles and sparklines from GeckoTerminal. The board is rebuilt from those sources every five minutes. Nothing is typed in by hand.

Why is high turnover a red flag?

On a DEX the price is simply where the last trades left the pool. If a pool with $100K of liquidity reports $10M of volume, that depth changed hands a hundred times in a day. Real demand rarely does that. A loop of wash trades does, and the price it leaves behind is not a market price.

Which stocks are included?

Tokens that Jupiter tags with an issuer (xStocks, Ondo or Backpack) and that hold at least $5K of liquidity. Copies that reuse a real ticker without the issuer tag are dropped. Empty listings with no pool are left out.

Does it cost anything?

No. No wallet, no sign up, no token. Open the terminal and read.

Is this financial advice?

No. Strak is a reading instrument. High turnover says the reported volume doesn't fit the pool, not what the stock will do next.
